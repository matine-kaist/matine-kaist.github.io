This project aims to improve the acoustic characteristics of thin-film speakers built from dielectric elastomers.
When a high voltage is applied across the electrodes sandwiching the elastomer film, Maxwell stress squeezes the film, which then deforms in a (visco-)hyperelastic manner.
The project models this deformation in response to the electrical input as a nonlinear ODE, providing a physics-based simulation of how the film responds to changes in the driving signal.
Building on this model, the project develops methods to reduce the nonlinear distortion of the radiated sound.

To validate the model, elastic speakers were fabricated and driven, and both the film deformation and the radiated sound were measured with microphones and laser Doppler vibrometers (LDVs).
The measurements confirmed that the model-based methods reduce distortion in the produced sound.
The figures above show two types of the thin-film speaker arrays used in the experiments.
The array on the left also includes a photonic nanocrystal gel that changes color with strain, visualizing how far the elastomer stretches.
More details can be found in [the paper](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10741525).
