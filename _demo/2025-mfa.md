This project, carried out at MIT in collaboration with the Museum of Fine Arts (MFA), Boston, digitizes historic musical instruments and recreates them in playable form to uncover new insights into how they were made and how they sounded.

The project was featured in [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2026/recreating-forms-sounds-historic-musical-instruments-0305).

The instruments are digitized with CT scanners and characterized through non-destructive acoustic measurements.
Since no measurement campaign can cover every playing condition, numerical simulations are used to infer how the instruments sound, and the simulations are validated by reproducing the measured results.

Beyond acoustics, the project is multidisciplinary, supporting research in materials science and archaeology. See the MIT News article and the video above for more details.

The project is ongoing.
Beyond this ocarina, the museum's collection spans a wide range of wind, string, and percussion instruments, and work on them continues. Stay tuned for future updates!
